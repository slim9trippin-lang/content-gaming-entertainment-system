# What kind of game would the Necronomicon make?

## A first note: the source has two readings

The Necronomicon isn't a single work — it's a fictional grimoire that lives across two very different traditions:

- **Lovecraftian:** the "Mad Arab" Abdul Alhazred's tome of forbidden knowledge from H.P. Lovecraft's mythos — cosmic horror, sanity-shattering revelations, scholar-protagonists undone by what they uncover.
- **Evil Dead-style:** the campy *Book of the Dead* from Sam Raimi's films — possession, gore, a chainsaw-wielding everyman, horror-comedy.

These produce wildly different game recommendations. The breakdown below assumes the **Lovecraftian reading** (which is the dominant one when the word is used cold). If you want the Evil Dead reading, say so and I'll re-score — that one points hard at Arena Brawler + Roguelike (basically *Hades* with deadites).

## The mechanical DNA (Lovecraftian reading)

- **Protagonist's relationship to space:** investigation through small, contained, oppressive locations — Miskatonic University libraries, Innsmouth, Arkham, abandoned manors. Spaces feel *wrong.*
- **Protagonist's relationship to conflict:** outlast, comprehend, flee. The Lovecraftian protagonist is *not* a fighter. Most stories, they don't survive what they uncover.
- **Protagonist's economy of power:** inverted. Knowledge corrupts. The more you learn, the closer you are to madness, dissolution, or possession. The "stat" the protagonist accumulates is *damage.*
- **Pacing rhythm:** slow-burn, atmospheric, dread-building. Discovery is the engine.
- **What goes wrong on failure:** madness, death, transformation. Often all three.

## Skeleton scoring

| Skeleton              | Verdict        | One-line reason                                                                  |
|-----------------------|----------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Survival Horror       | Clean fit      | Outmatched protagonist, scarce resources, dread is the dominant emotion          |
| Roguelike             | Clean fit      | "Knowledge corrupts" maps perfectly to meta-progression where each death teaches |
| Stealth               | Strained fit   | Cultists/eldritch beings as detection sources work, but Lovecraftian protagonists aren't trained infiltrators |
| Top-Down Tactician    | Strained fit   | *Call of Cthulhu* tabletop is structured this way, but the dread doesn't survive stat-and-inventory rhythm |
| Arena Brawler         | Wrong tool     | Cosmic horror's premise is that the protagonist *can't* fight back              |
| Turn-Based Strategist | Wrong tool     | Menu-driven combat is tonally lethal to Lovecraftian dread                       |
| Controidvania         | Wrong tool     | Kinesthetic flow contradicts the "wrong, slow, oppressive" feel of the source    |

## Recommendation

The two strongest fits are **Survival Horror** and **Roguelike** — and they're stronger *together* than apart.

**Survival Horror** is the obvious match. Bloodborne (in spirit), *Eternal Darkness*, *Call of Cthulhu: Dark Corners of the Earth*, *Amnesia: The Dark Descent*, *Signalis* — there's a whole tradition here. Vulnerability, scarce sanity/ammo, contained space, lore-through-fragments. This is the safe, well-lit road.

**Roguelike** is the more *interesting* fit. The premise practically writes itself: each run is a new investigator (a different academic, antiquarian, or unlucky witness) who finds the Necronomicon and tries to read further than the last one did. They die, go mad, or get possessed. The meta-progression unlocks *actual pages of the book* for the player to read between runs — a literal in-game Necronomicon that grows as you fail. *That* is a hook with real legs.

The hybrid — **Roguelike Survival Horror** — is *Returnal* / *Darkest Dungeon*-shaped and might be the answer. Run-based dread, cumulative knowledge, no protagonist gets out alive but the *book* (and the player's understanding) keeps growing.

## What I'd do next

If you want a full breakdown, pick one of:
- **Survival Horror** (the focused, atmosphere-first version)
- **Roguelike** (the structurally innovative version with the page-unlock meta-loop)
- **Survival Horror + Roguelike hybrid** (most ambitious)
