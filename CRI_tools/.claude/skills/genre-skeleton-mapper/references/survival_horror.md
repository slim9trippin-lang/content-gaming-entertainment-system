# Survival Horror — Skeleton Reference

**One-line vibe:** the protagonist is *outmatched*. Resources are scarce, threats are persistent, and the dominant emotion is dread, not power.

**Lineage:** *Resident Evil* (1, 7), *Silent Hill*, *Amnesia*, *Alien: Isolation*, *Outlast*, *Signalis*, *The Last of Us* (loose).

## Core mechanical DNA

- **Movement model:** deliberate, often *deliberately clunky*. Tank controls or weighty modern movement. Movement should feel like *commitment*, not flow.
- **Combat model:** combat exists but is a *failure state of first resort*. Ammo is scarce, damage is high, healing is rare. The protagonist usually wants to *avoid* fights, not engage.
- **Progression model:** minimal mechanical progression. The protagonist may gain a key item or two, marginal stat boosts, occasional weapons — but they end the game roughly as vulnerable as they started.
- **World structure:** often a single connected location (a mansion, a town, a station, a derelict ship) explored over the course of the game. Backtracking is common; key items unlock previously locked doors.
- **Failure state:** death is punishing — long reload, lost progress, ammo wasted. The save system itself is often a resource (typewriter ribbons, tape recorders).

## The translation job

This is the skeleton where *vulnerability* is the design lock-in.

1. **What is the protagonist *afraid* of?** (a specific monster, an environment, a person, a memory) → becomes the persistent threat.
2. **What's scarce in the source's world?** (food, water, ammunition, allies, sanity, time) → becomes the central resource.
3. **What's the contained space?** (mansion, hospital, town, ship, district, mind) → becomes the explorable map.
4. **What does the protagonist *not* know?** → becomes the discovery loop (notes, journals, environmental storytelling).
5. **What's the protagonist's last-resort tool?** (a single bullet, a flare, a memory) → becomes the panic-button mechanic.

## Suits source material when

- The protagonist is fundamentally *not* a fighter (literary horror, ordinary-person-in-crisis stories).
- The threat is overwhelming and persistent.
- Atmosphere matters more than action.
- The world is contained — a few square blocks, a single building, a small island.
- The story's pleasure is in *dread*, not victory.

## Strains when

- The protagonist is competent at combat (Brawler territory).
- The world is open and traversal-driven (Controidvania).
- The story is fast-paced or comedic.
- The supporting cast is large and active (most JRPG-shaped sources).

## Mixer affinity

- **Poor pairing with most skeletons.** Survival Horror's tone collapses when blended with confident combat or action loops.
- Reasonable pairing with Stealth — both share the "outmatched protagonist" premise.
- Possible pairing with Roguelike *only* if the run framing supports it (rare; *Returnal* leans this way but isn't really horror).
- Avoid mixing as host or guest with Brawler, Controidvania, or Turn-Based.

## Mapping checklist

- [ ] Persistent threat (the source's antagonist translated into a stalker / unkillable enemy / environment)
- [ ] Central scarce resource (what does the protagonist run out of?)
- [ ] Save system (and whether saving itself is a resource)
- [ ] Map: a single contained space with at least 3 sub-zones
- [ ] Key item progression (3–5 items that unlock specific doors/areas)
- [ ] Note/lore system (how does the protagonist learn what's happening?)
- [ ] Panic-button / last-resort mechanic
- [ ] At least one moment of *forced powerlessness* (no combat option, only escape)
