## 10. KNOWLEDGE TIERS (Information Gating)
*This is the layer the genre stress-tests hardest. Fort built his entire book around it.*

- **Public knowledge:** "Sometimes fish or frogs fall from the sky during storms. The weather is strange. Science explains all of it." This is the dominant register — what newspapers report and citizens believe.
- **Specialist knowledge (scientists, meteorologists, geologists):** "Anomalous falls do occur. They are explained by waterspouts, tornadoes, atmospheric updrafts carrying small organisms aloft and depositing them elsewhere. Volcanic dust accounts for unusual sunsets. Coincidence accounts for the rest." This is Fort's *target* — what he calls Dogmatic Science.
- **Hidden / forbidden knowledge (Forteans, occultists, conspiracy researchers):** "The conventional explanations don't fit the data. Frogs of identical species fall in single events; ice falls in cloudless skies; manufactured objects appear with no source. There is a region above the earth from which these things originate. The data has been 'damned' — excluded from the scientific record because it cannot be assimilated."
- **Author / GM only:** The Super-Sargasso Sea is real. It is also infrastructure for an older order that uses Earth as resource and pasture. The "owners" are not necessarily malevolent; the relationship is closer to husbandry. The disappearances are not random. There is a schedule.

## 11. DEPLOYMENT (Narrative & Encounter Use)
- **Recurring narrative roles:**

  - Antagonist-as-setting (the Sea opposes scientific certainty; characters who insist on explanations are weakened)
  - Conveyor of MacGuffins (the manufactured-object deposits are how impossible items enter the game world)
  - Threshold (player ascents become tonal turning points)
- **Sample encounter compositions:**
  - Solo investigator + townspeople + a single anomalous fall (Heroic tier)
  - Investigator network + government cover-up + multi-site pattern (Paragon tier)
  - Cult of the Sea + a "ship" sighting + an attempted controlled ascent (Epic tier)
- **Plot hooks / quest seeds:**
  1. A fishing village reports the same species of fish falling each summer. The species lives nowhere on earth.
  2. A small object — clearly forged, clearly ancient, clearly impossible — is found in a child's garden. Provenance investigation leads to similar finds across three continents in the same week.
  3. A scientist who tried to track Krakatoa's atmospheric dust through 1890 has gone missing. Her notebooks describe "currents."
  4. A community has built a religion around the Falls. Their calendar predicts the next one. They are correct.
  5. An object the player owns is *taken up* mid-conversation. The player has been marked.
- **Named exemplars:**
  - The 1883 Krakatoa atmospheric phenomena (Fort's case study)
  - The fish-falls of Mountain Ash, Wales (frequently cited Fortean event)
  - The Tunguska event (post-Fort but fits)
  - Charles Fort himself — the *only* documented observer who built a cosmology around the Sea
- **Templates / mutations / hybrids:** the Sea pairs naturally with Lovecraftian cosmology (it's the *infrastructure* that the Old Ones use); with Theosophical "astral planes" (it's the lower edge); with modern UFO mythology (it's where the craft come from); with Christian eschatology (it's the source of manna and signs)

## Stress-test notes (extractor commentary)

What this extraction revealed about the template:

1. **§10 Knowledge Tiers is the load-bearing column for this genre.** Every other section had to be filled with reference back to which tier the claim sits in. The four-tier structure (Public / Specialist / Hidden / Author-only) maps onto Fort's epistemology almost perfectly. Recommend keeping this section's structure exactly as-is; it was the only one that didn't strain.

2. **§4 Mind partially broke.** Fort never gives the Sea agency in *Book of the Damned* — agency only emerges in later books with "we are property." For a strict single-source extraction, §4 had to be filled with [INFERRED] notes pointing forward to extra-source material. Suggests a possible §4a sub-field: "agency status (explicit / implicit / cross-source-only / none)."

3. **§2 Form needed loosening.** Designed for creatures; a region/setting entity has no "body schema" in any meaningful sense. Recommend adding a "this section [N/A for Setting entities]" affordance, or splitting the template now into Entity/Faction/Location/Item/Event as previously discussed. The single-template-flexes approach worked here but only with heavy [N/A]-ing.

4. **§12 Links did the most useful work.** This entity is *defined* by its connections — every anomalous fall is a link, every disappearance is a link, every later author who built on Fort is a link. The Links section produced the richest output relative to its size.

5. **Source-tier handling worked.** T3 (narrative source) with ~60% inferred fields produced a usable game-design artifact. The fill-flags carried the weight as designed.

6. **Copyright compliance held.** Only one direct under-15-word quote ("I accept only") was used. Everything else paraphrased. Fort's prose is distinctive enough that mimicry would have been a real risk — the discipline mattered.
